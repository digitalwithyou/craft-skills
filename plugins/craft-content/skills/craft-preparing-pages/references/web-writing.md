# Web writing rules (all languages)

People scan before they read. NN/g found measured usability was 58% higher for concise text, 47% higher for scannable text and 27% higher for objective rather than promotional text. Everything below serves those three.

## Structure

- **Front-load**: the answer or the main point first, in the page, in each section, in each paragraph. Context and nuance follow.
- One idea per paragraph; 2–4 lines on desktop. A paragraph that needs a "furthermore" is two paragraphs.
- Lists for parallel items (steps, features, requirements), not for arguments that need connecting words.
- Write about half of what a printed brochure would say. Cut sentences the visitor wouldn't miss.
- Every section answers a question the visitor has (from the brief), in the order they'd ask it.

## Sentences and words

- Plain language, roughly B1 level for general audiences; B2B can be more technical, but still define jargon once.
- Average sentence ≤ 15–20 words; vary length; no sentence over ~30 words.
- Active voice and concrete subjects: who does what.
- Address the visitor (u/je, you, vous) more than "we". Describe what they get, not what you offer.
- Verbs over nominalizations. Specific nouns over abstract ones.
- Use the client's own words from discovery: how their customers describe the problem.

## Proof over adjectives

Replace every evaluative adjective with a fact, or delete it:

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "jarenlange ervaring" | "sinds 1998, 400 renovaties in Mechelen en omgeving" (if verified) |
| "snelle service" | "antwoord binnen één werkdag" |
| "kwalitatieve producten" | the material, the guarantee, the certification |

No fact available → `[TE BEVESTIGEN: …]` or leave the claim out. Never invent one.

## Headings

- A heading tells the scanner what the section gives them. Prefer statements or the visitor's question over clever slogans.
- Sentence case in Dutch, French and (default) English.
- One h1 per page; descend without skipping levels; the visual size comes from the design, not the level.
- Keep headings short (≤ ~8 words) and unique on the page.

## Links and buttons

- Link text says where it goes or what happens: "Bekijk de prijzen voor renovatie", not "klik hier" or "lees meer".
- Buttons start with a verb and name the result: "Vraag een offerte aan", "Plan een gesprek", "Download de brochure (pdf, 2 MB)".
- One primary button per section at most.
- Internal links: link from this page to the next logical step, and propose links *to* this page from related pages in the report.

## SEO fields

- **Title**: ≤ ~60 characters, main topic first, brand last ("Renovatie rijwoning in Mechelen | <Merk>"). Unique per page and per language.
- **Meta description**: ~120–155 characters, what the visitor gets plus a reason to click; not a keyword list.
- **Slug**: short, lowercase, hyphens, in the site's language.
- Write for the search query from the brief, in natural language. Use the main term in the title, h1 and first paragraph, then write normally.
- If the site uses SEOmatic, fill its fields; otherwise the site's SEO fields (see the block catalog).

## Alt text

- Describe what the image shows *in this context*, in ~5–15 words, without "afbeelding van". Example: "Vakman plaatst houten ramen in een rijwoning".
- Decorative images (patterns, mood shots without information): empty alt text, marked decorative.
- Images with text in them: the alt text contains that text.
- Write alt text per language.

## Accessibility of content

- Meaning never depends on color or position alone ("klik op de groene knop rechts").
- Link texts are unique and meaningful out of context.
- Abbreviations explained at first use.
- Tables only for tabular data, with header rows.
- Video: captions and a text summary; audio: a transcript.
